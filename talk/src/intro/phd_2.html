PDL: pairwise difference learning, with uncertainty for free

PDL classifier: wraps any probabilistic classifier; better accuracy on 100+ tabular benchmarks, largest gains below N = 500

Weighted PDL: learned anchor weights, 3–5× faster inference without retraining

Uncertainty: the anchors act as a free ensemble, splitting aleatoric vs epistemic uncertainty; a deterministic model becomes calibrated

Scale: pip install pdll; benchmark of 99 OpenML datasets on about 700 CPUs (LRZ, SLURM)

Backup. PDL learns to predict the difference between pairs; at inference a query is compared with anchors whose labels are known, and the anchor votes are aggregated. Weighted PDL learns anchor weights (3–5× speed-up). UQ-PDL (Machine Learning 115(1), 2026): the anchor ensemble is a free source of variability, which decomposes total uncertainty into aleatoric and epistemic parts without an explicit ensemble; a deterministic base learner becomes a calibrated first-order predictor, and a probabilistic one becomes second-order.
