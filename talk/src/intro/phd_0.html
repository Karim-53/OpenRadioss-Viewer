Learning by Pairing

Instead of mapping one input to its label, learn from pairs: how two examples differ. It makes models more accurate, calibrated and explainable when labels are scarce, and the same idea carries over to LLM agents.

Validated at Porsche on crash-test data, where every labelled example is a destroyed prototype. ↓ one slide per paper

~60 s. "My PhD is on learning by pairing. Instead of mapping one input to its label, the model learns from pairs, from how two examples differ: n points become n-squared training signals. That helps most where labels are expensive, which was exactly my situation at Porsche. The thesis has three parts. Explain: Compare-xAI, a benchmark of functional tests for explanation methods, showing that none of the popular ones is reliable on all four dimensions. Predict: pairwise difference learning, which also lets us split uncertainty into aleatoric and epistemic parts without training an ensemble; that's in the Machine Learning journal this year. And agents: the Autodidact Agent, which learns an undocumented engineering method from its own cases and beats GPT-5 with retrieval on engineering and cardiology benchmarks."
